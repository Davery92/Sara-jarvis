# TWO-A-DAY POWERBUILDING RECOMP PROGRAM

**8-Week Training Block · 4 Days/Week · AM Strength + PM Bodybuilding**
**MON Chest · TUE Back · WED Shoulders · THU Legs**
**In effect from Monday 2026-09-14.** Source: David, 2026-09-14 (verbatim below, reformatted to markdown).

> The morning session trains performance: one heavy primary lift, low fatigue, high quality.
> The afternoon session trains physique: targeted volume, controlled proximity to failure, progressive overload.

## 1. Program Overview

This is not two separate programs. It is one powerbuilding program split across two daily training windows so strength work and hypertrophy work can each be performed while relatively fresh.

| Day | AM: Strength | PM: Hypertrophy | Primary Goal |
|---|---|---|---|
| Monday | NewTech Flat Press | Chest + triceps | Horizontal pressing strength / chest growth |
| Tuesday | Deadlift | Lats + upper back + biceps | Hinge strength / back development |
| Wednesday | Overhead Press | Delts + arms | Vertical pressing strength / shoulder width |
| Thursday | Squat | Quads + hamstrings + calves | Squat strength / leg growth |

### Non-negotiable rules
- AM sessions stay short: one primary lift, usually 3–5 challenging work sets total.
- No failed reps or grinders on the morning lifts. Most heavy work stays at or below RPE 8.
- PM compounds generally stop with 1–2 reps in reserve; isolation work can reach 0–2 RIR.
- Tuesday deadlift volume stays deliberately low so Thursday squats remain productive.
- Do not add extra AM bodybuilding work just because time is available. The split works because fatigue is controlled.

### Why shoulders move to Wednesday
Placing shoulders between back and legs creates roughly 48 hours between Tuesday deadlifts and Thursday squats. That is a much cleaner fatigue pattern than squatting the morning after deadlifting. Monday chest and Wednesday shoulders overlap somewhat through the triceps/front delts, but the spacing is much easier to manage.

### Starting Strength Anchors & Week 1 Loads
These are programming anchors, not claims that every estimated max has been formally tested. The Smith OHP estimate is deliberately provisional because 135 lb for 4×4 is the demonstrated data point; the program will validate the estimate through progression.

| Lift | Current anchor | Programming assumption | Week 1 top set | Week 1 backoff |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| NewTech Flat Press | 270 lb × 2–3 | 270 benchmark | 270 × 2 | 230 × 4–6 × 3 |
| Deadlift | ~365 lb 1RM | 365 1RM | 315 × 1–3 | 275 × 3–5 × 2 |
| Smith Machine OHP | 135 lb × 4 × 4 | ~180 e1RM | 145 × 2–4 | 130 × 4–6 × 3 |
| Squat | ~265 lb 1RM | 265 1RM | 225 × 2–4 | 200 × 4–6 × 3 |

Load convention: NewTech numbers are total plates across both sides. Smith OHP numbers should use the same convention you used when recording 135 lb so the log stays internally consistent.

## 6. Progression & 8-Week Structure

### AM progression — strength
The AM lifts use the exact loading plan below. Treat the listed numbers as targets, not obligations: if the prescribed top set would exceed RPE 8, repeat the previous successful load instead of forcing the increase. The estimates are anchors for programming; clean performance controls progression.

Example — NewTech: 3 plates/side × 2 → × 3 → × 4, then add a small plate and return toward the lower end of the range.
Do not force a load increase if bar/machine speed, technique, or recovery clearly deteriorates.

### PM progression — hypertrophy
Use double progression. Keep the same load until all prescribed sets reach the top of the rep range with the intended RIR, then increase the load and build the reps again.
Example: incline DB press 3×6–10. Build 80 lb from 9/8/7 to 10/10/10, then move to the next load and rebuild.

### Block structure
| Weeks | Phase | Volume | Focus |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weeks 1–3 | Build | Normal volume | Learn recoverable workload; progress reps/load |
| Week 4 | Deload | ~50% PM sets | AM backoffs reduced; stay ~3–4 RIR PM |
| Weeks 5–7 | Build | Normal volume | Beat Weeks 1–3 with clean progression |
| Week 8 | Performance | Normal-to-slightly reduced | Rep PRs at familiar loads; no true max testing required |

## 7. Cardio, Recovery & Guardrails
- Cardio goes after the AM strength work, not before it.
- Use roughly 10–15 minutes of StairMaster or incline walking on 2–4 mornings per week, primarily at an easy-to-moderate aerobic effort.
- Avoid hard interval work around deadlift and squat sessions unless recovery is clearly excellent.
- Friday through Sunday contain no required lifting. Use the weekend to recover from Thursday legs.
- If Thursday squat performance repeatedly declines, reduce Tuesday deadlift backoff volume before changing the entire split.
- Persistent joint irritation, worsening sleep, falling performance, or unusual fatigue are signals to reduce volume rather than add more work.

## 8. Recomp Nutrition Integration
Does the new program change the meal-plan concept? Not fundamentally. It actually makes the meal timing we designed more useful because you now have two deliberate training windows to fuel.

A sensible starting training-day target remains approximately **2,760 kcal with 240 g protein, 270 g carbohydrate, and 80 g fat**. Treat this as a starting intake, not a permanent law: adjust from bodyweight/waist trends, gym performance, hunger, and recovery after several weeks of consistent data.

| Time | Meal / Activity | Target Macros | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5:00–5:30 AM | Wake / water | — | Hydration |
| 6:15–6:40 AM | Nurri + easy carbs | 30P / 38C / 3F | Fuel 7 AM strength session |
| 7:00 AM | AM strength + optional cardio | — | Heavy main lift |
| 8:00–8:15 AM | Office breakfast | 45P / 52C / 15F | Recovery + replenish |
| 11:30 AM | Main pre-workout meal | 50P / 75C / 12F | Largest pre-PM carb feeding |
| 1:00 PM | PM bodybuilding | — | Main volume session |
| 2:15–2:30 PM | Post-workout meal | 50P / 65C / 15F | Recovery |
| 6:30–7:00 PM | Dinner | 65P / 40C / 35F | Final meal / daily completion |
| **DAILY TOTAL** | — | **240P / 270C / 80F** | **≈ 2,760 kcal** |

### What changes with the finalized program?
- Keep the fast morning fuel. A Nurri plus banana/rice cake/honey remains ideal because the AM workout is heavy but intentionally low-volume.
- Keep the largest carb allocation around the 1 PM bodybuilding session. That session creates substantially more repeated-set glycogen demand than a few heavy AM work sets.
- Do not automatically increase calories just because there are two sessions. Start with the planned recomp intake and let performance/body-composition trends decide whether more food is needed.
- Tuesday deadlift and Thursday legs are the days where carbohydrate availability matters most for lower-body performance. The existing pre-workout meal already covers this well; if you ever redistribute carbs, bias them toward those days rather than adding calories blindly.
- Keep protein distributed across the day. The existing five-feed structure provides repeated high-quality protein doses without requiring a late-night meal.

Bottom line: the training plan changes the purpose of the meals more than it changes the meals themselves. Morning food supports strength; the 11:30 meal supports the main hypertrophy session; the afternoon and dinner feed recovery.
